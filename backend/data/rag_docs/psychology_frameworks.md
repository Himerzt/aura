# AURA — Psychology Frameworks Reference

AURA uses 8 evidence-based psychological intervention frameworks. The system selects the appropriate framework based on the user's detected psychological pattern and mood state.

---

## 1. 80/20 Pareto Rule

**Trigger:** Too many tasks, unsure what to prioritize.

**Concept:** Focus on the 20% of tasks that produce 80% of results. Identify the highest-leverage actions and eliminate or defer the rest.

**When to use:** When the user feels overwhelmed by a long to-do list or doesn't know where to start.

**Task approach:** Narrow down to 1-2 high-impact tasks. Avoid busywork.

---

## 2. Behavioral Activation

**Trigger:** Emotional numbness, lack of motivation, waiting for inspiration.

**Concept:** Action precedes motivation, not the other way around. Start with the smallest possible action to break the inertia cycle.

**When to use:** When the user says "I don't feel like doing anything" or "I'm waiting to feel motivated."

**Task approach:** Start ridiculously small. A 5-minute walk beats a 0-minute rest.

---

## 3. Implementation Intention

**Trigger:** Knows what to do but keeps forgetting or procrastinating.

**Concept:** Create "When X happens, I will do Y" plans that link actions to specific times, places, and triggers. Reduces decision fatigue at the moment of action.

**When to use:** When the user has clear tasks but struggles with execution.

**Task approach:** Tasks must include: trigger, action, duration, location. Example: "When I finish my morning coffee, I will write 10 minutes in my journal at my desk."

---

## 4. Habit Stacking

**Trigger:** Wants to build a new habit but can't find a slot in the schedule.

**Concept:** Attach new habits to existing ones using the formula: "After [current habit], I will [new habit]."

**When to use:** When the user wants to establish routines but planning feels overwhelming.

**Task approach:** Stack new behavior onto an existing anchor habit. Keep it tiny and specific.

---

## 5. Self-Compassion

**Trigger:** Shame spiral, missed many days, self-criticism.

**Concept:** Treat yourself as you would treat a good friend who is struggling. Self-criticism shrinks capacity; self-compassion expands it.

**When to use:** After a setback, missed days, or when the user is being harsh on themselves.

**Task approach:** Reframe failure as data. Suggest one small, kind action rather than a corrective punishment.

---

## 6. Progress Principle

**Trigger:** Lost motivation, can't see any progress.

**Concept:** Small, consistent wins fuel intrinsic motivation. Track progress visibly to make invisible progress visible.

**When to use:** When the user feels like they're not getting anywhere or their efforts aren't paying off.

**Task approach:** Acknowledge past wins. Suggest one action that builds on existing momentum.

---

## 7. Two-Minute Rule

**Trigger:** Inertia — knows what to do but can't start.

**Concept:** If a task takes less than 2 minutes, do it immediately. For longer tasks, commit only to the first 2 minutes to overcome starting resistance.

**When to use:** When the user is stuck at the starting line, even for simple tasks.

**Task approach:** Make the first step absurdly small. The goal is to start, not to finish.

---

## 8. Dunning-Kruger Effect

**Trigger:** Wants to give up (valley of despair) or overconfident.

**Concept:** The valley of despair is a predictable stage of skill acquisition, not a sign of failure. Persistence through this phase leads to genuine competence.

**When to use:** When the user feels like they're getting worse despite effort, or conversely, thinks they know everything already.

**Task approach:** Normalize the learning curve. Suggest continuing at a sustainable pace.

---

## Framework Selection Logic

| Mood State | Likely Framework |
|-----------|-----------------|
| overwhelmed | 80_20_pareto, Behavioral Activation |
| numb | Behavioral Activation, Two-Minute Rule |
| anxious | Implementation Intention, Habit Stacking |
| stable | Progress Principle, any framework |
| energized | Habit Stacking, Progress Principle |

| Pattern Detected | Likely Framework |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| learned_helplessness | Behavioral Activation, Two-Minute Rule |
| shame_spiral | Self-Compassion |
| avoidance_loop | Implementation Intention |
| analysis_paralysis | Two-Minute Rule, 80_20_pareto |
| valley_of_despair | Dunning-Kruger |

## Important Notes

- AURA is a coaching and support tool, NOT a medical or therapy product.
- AURA does not replace professional mental health care.
- Crisis situations are flagged and routed to support resources.
- All frameworks are applied as coaching nudges, not clinical interventions.
